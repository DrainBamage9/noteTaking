### Tangent Planes and Linear Approximations

In single-variable calculus, the tangent line approximates a curve at a given point.

In multivariable calc, this is instead the tangent plane at a given point.

If $z=f(x,y)$, then the tangent plane at an arbitrary point (a,b) is:

$$z=f(a,b) + f_x(a,b)(x-a) + f_y(a,b)(y-b)$$

This gives the linear approximation: $f(x,y)\approx L(x,y)$ near (a,b) where $L(x,y)$ is defined by the equation above.

#### Example
Given the plane defined by the function. Find tangent plane at (1,2).

$$f(x,y) = x^2 + y^2$$


Step 1. Compute partial derivatives.

$$f_x = 2x, \quad f_y=2y$$

Find its values at (1,2):

$$f_x=2,\quad f_y=4$$

Step 2. Evaluate the original function

$$f(1,2)=1+4=5$$

Step 3. Plug into tangent plane formula

$$z=5+2(x-1)+4(y-2)$$

Simplify

$$z=2x+4y-5$$

### Why do we are about linear approximations?
In high-level controls and engineering, evaluating a heavy quadratic function may not be realistic. This could be for a multitude of reasons, most likely its comnplexity. We simply make a linear approximation about of point of interest and calculate the level of error we may have.

# The Tangent and Velocity Problems
The tangent is a line that touches a curve of another line at an arbitrary point of contact. It will follow the same direction as that sliver of curve at the point of contact. 

## Tangent Line Problem
**Goal**: Find the slope of a curve at a single point.
Average slope between two points:

$$m = \frac{f(x_2) - f(x_1)}{x_2-x_1}$$

For a point $x=a$:

$$ m = \frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h}$$

where $h$ is the change, or difference in $x$.

### Tangent Line Slope
The slope of a tangent line is the **limit**.

$$ m = \lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h}$$

- $h\to 0$: Our difference to our point of interest is decreasing, approaching zero.
- Thie becomes the foundation of a derivative.

### Tangent Line Equation
Point-Slope form:

$$y-f(a)=m(x-a)$$

where:

- $m$ = tangent slope
- $(a,f(a))$ = point on a curve, somne arbitrary point of interest. 

## Velocity Problem
Average Velocity:

$$v_{avg} = \frac{\text{change in position}}{\text{change in time}}$$

$$v_{avg} = \frac{s(t_2) - s(t_1)}{t_2 - t_1}$$

Instantaneous Velocity:

$$v=\lim_{h\to 0} \frac{s(t+h)-s(t)}{h}$$

## Key Idea
Both problems use the same concept of the rate of change is equal to the change in output over the change in input. Or how much does our output change given a change in input?

$$\text{Rate of change} = \frac{\text{change in output}}{\text{change in input}}$$

- Tangent Problem $\to$ rate of change of a function
- Velocity Problem $\to$ rate of change of position

# Reduction of a Simple Distributed Loading

## Distributed Loading

A distributed load applies force continuously over a length of a body, such as a beam or plate. A load is something like the wind on the face of a sign, or water within a tank. This works for loading along a single axis. Load intensity $w$ is force per unit length. (n/m)

The distributed load can be replaced by an equivalent resultant force acting at the **centroid of the load diagram** defined by the variable $\bar x$ in unit length.

## Variable Definition
- $\bar x$ Is the location of the force centroid. Or where an equivilent force would act.
- $F_R$ Is the resultant force generated by the load.

## Resultant Force
The resultant force equals the **area** under the load-intensity diagram.

### General Resultant 

For any distributed load defiend by an equation:
$$
F_R=\int _L w(x)dx = \int _A dA=A
$$
$$
\bar{x}=\frac{\int xw(x)\,dx}{F_R} = \frac{\int _A xdA}{\int _A dA}
$$

For multiple load shapes:

$$
\bar{x}=\frac{\sum F_i x_i}{\sum F_i}
$$

where $F_i$ is each component's resultant and $x_i$ is its location. We can use $A$ (area) here as more of placeholder, think about it as the area under a curve where the curve is defined by our load function $w$. 

### Uniform Loading

For constant intensity $w$ over length $L$:

$$
F_R=wL
$$

The resultant acts at the midpoint:

$$
\bar{x}=\frac{L}{2}
$$

### Triangular Loading

For intensity varying **linearly** from zero to $w_{\max}$:

$$
F_R=\frac12 w_{\max}L
$$

$$
\bar{x} = \frac{L}{3} \\\\ \text{with regards to the maximum-load end}
$$

### Trapezoidal Loading

For intensity varying linearly from $w_1$ to $w_2$:

$$
F_R=\frac{w_1+w_2}{2}L
$$

The resultant location measured from the $w_1$ end is:

$$
\bar{x}=\frac{L(w_1+2w_2)}{3(w_1+w_2)}
$$


## Procedure

1. Divide the loading diagram into simple shapes.
2. Calculate each resultant from its area.
3. Locate each resultant at its centroid.
4. Combine the forces and calculate the resultant location using moments.

# Example Problems: Reduction of Distributed Loading

## Example 1: Triangular Loading

A beam of length $6\,\text{m}$ supports a triangular distributed load that increases linearly from zero at $A$ to $12\,\text{kN/m}$ at $B$.

Determine the equivalent resultant force and its location measured from $A$.

### Solution

**1. Resultant Force**

The resultant equals the area of the triangular load diagram.

$$
F_R=\frac12 w_{max}L
$$

$$
F_R=\frac12(12)(6)
$$

$$
\boxed{F_R=36\,\text{kN}}
$$

The force acts downward.

**2. Centroid**

For a triangular load increasing from zero, the centroid is located one-third of the length from the maximum-intensity end.

Measured from $A$:

$$
\bar{x}=\frac23L
$$

$$
\bar{x}=\frac23(6)
$$

$$
\boxed{\bar{x}=4\,\text{m from }A}
$$

**Answer:** Replace the distributed load with a $36\,\text{kN}$ downward force acting $4\,\text{m}$ from $A$.

## Example 2: Distributed Load Defined by a Function

A $4\,\text{m}$ beam supports a downward distributed load defined by

$$
w(x)=x^2+1\quad\text{kN/m}
$$

where $x$ is measured in meters from the left end $A$.

Determine the equivalent resultant force and its centroid.

### Solution

**1. Resultant Force**

Integrate the load intensity over the beam's length.

$$
F_R=\int_0^4 w(x)\,dx
$$

Substitute the function:

$$
F_R=\int_0^4(x^2+1)\,dx
$$

$$
F_R=\left[\frac{x^3}{3}+x\right]_0^4
$$

$$
F_R=\frac{64}{3}+4
$$

$$
\boxed{F_R=25.33\,\text{kN}}
$$

The resultant force acts downward.

### 2. Centroid

Take moments of the distributed load about $A$.

$$
\bar{x}=\frac{\int_0^4 xw(x)\,dx}{F_R}
$$

Substitute:

$$
\bar{x}=\frac{\int_0^4x(x^2+1)\,dx}{25.33}
$$

$$
\bar{x}=\frac{\int_0^4(x^3+x)\,dx}{25.33}
$$

Integrate:

$$
\bar{x}=\left[\frac{x^4}{4}+\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^4=72
$$


Solve for the centroid using the exact resultant $F_R=76/3$:

$$
\bar{x}=\frac{72}{25.33}
$$

$$
\boxed{\bar{x}=2.84\,\text{m from }A}
$$

**Answer:** Replace the nonlinear distributed load with a $25.33\,\text{kN}$ downward force acting $2.84\,\text{m}$ from $A$.

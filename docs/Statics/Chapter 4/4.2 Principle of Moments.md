# Principle of Moments

There is a theorem used in mechanics called Varignon's theorem, named after Pierre Varignon that states that "The Moment of a force about about a points is equal to the sum of the moment of the components of the force about the point." Which is really fancy way of saying the following:
$$
M_O = F_xy + F_yx
$$

## Example

A force of $F = 500N$ is applied at point $A$, which is location 5m from our origin $O$ at an angle of $30\degree$ above the $x$-axis. The force acts at $60\degree$ above the $x$-axis.

Find the moment of $F$ about $O$ using **Varignon's Theorem**

1. Resolve Force into its components
$$
F_x = 500\cos{60\degree}=\bf{250N}
$$
$$
F_y = 500\sin{60\degree}=\bf{433N}
$$

Now do the same thing with the position of $A$

$$
x = 5\cos{30\degree}=\bf{4.33m}
$$
$$
y = 5\sin{30\degree}=\bf{2.50m}
$$
2. Find the moments generate by each dimension.

$$
M_{F_x} = -F_xy
$$
$$
M_{F_x} = -250\cdot{}2.50 
$$
$$
\boxed{M_{F_x}=-625\ N\cdot{}m}
$$
Now you may ask, why is x negative here? Draw the problem out, the $F_x$ is trying to rotate the member in a clockwise direction, which is inherently negative.

Now we find the moment generated in the y-dimension.
$$
M_{F_y}=F_yx
$$
$$
M_{F_y}=433\cdot{}4.33
$$
$$
\boxed{M_{F_y}=1875N\cdot{}m}
$$
3. Add them together

$$
M_O=M_{F_y}+M_{F_x}=1875-625=\boxed{1250N\cdot{}m}
$$

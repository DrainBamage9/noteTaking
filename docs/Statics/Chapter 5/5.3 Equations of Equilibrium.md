# Equations of Equilibrium

## 2D Equilibrium Equations

For a rigid body in planar equilibrium:

$$
\sum F_x=0,\qquad \sum F_y=0
$$

$$
\sum M_O=0
$$

These equations determine unknown reaction forces and couple moments.

## Applying the Equations

### Force Equations

Resolve angled forces into Cartesian components:

$$
F_x=F\cos\theta,\qquad F_y=F\sin\theta
$$

Assign signs according to the chosen coordinate system.

### Moment Equation

The sum of moments about any point (O) must equal zero.

$$
\sum M_O=0
$$

Choose a moment center that eliminates as many unknown forces as possible. A force whose line of action passes through (O) creates zero moment about (O).

## Alternative Equilibrium Formulations

The three equations can be replaced by equivalent sets, provided they remain independent:

1. $\sum F_x=0,\ \sum M_A=0,\ \sum M_B=0$, where the line (AB) is not perpendicular to the (x)-axis.
2. $\sum F_y=0,\ \sum M_A=0,\ \sum M_B=0$, where the line (AB) is not perpendicular to the (y)-axis.

These alternatives are useful when force components are difficult to resolve.

## Procedure

1. Draw a complete free-body diagram.
2. Establish coordinate axes and label unknown reactions.
3. Select a moment center that eliminates unknowns.
4. Apply $\sum M_O=0$ first when convenient.
5. Apply $\sum F_x=0$ and $\sum F_y=0$.
6. Solve the equations and interpret negative answers as directions opposite to those assumed.

## Determinacy

- **Statically determinate:** All unknown reactions can be solved using the independent equilibrium equations.
- **Statically indeterminate:** There are more unknown reactions than independent equilibrium equations; additional compatibility or deformation equations are required.
- **Unstable:** The supports do not adequately prevent rigid-body motion.

# Two and Three Force Members
The solutions to some problems can be simplified by recognizing members that are subject to 2 or 3 forces.

## Two-Force Members
If only two forces are acting on an object, in order to satisfy both the $ \sum M=0,\quad\sum F=0 $ conditions. The two forces must have the same magnitude, act in opposite directions, be collinear with the line connecting them (draw a line from A to B, they must act parallel to this line). 

## Three-Force Members
If exactly three forces are acting on an object it is called a three-force member. Moment equilibrium can be satisfied only if the forces are parallel or concurrent. Meaning:

- Concurrent: If the three forces are not parallel, their lines of action must _intersect_ at a common point. (Draw a line along each force, all 3 forces must meet at some point, forming a triangle)
- Parallel: If all three forces are parallel their lines of action do not need to intersect. Their magnitudes and locations must still satisfy both force and moment equilibrium.

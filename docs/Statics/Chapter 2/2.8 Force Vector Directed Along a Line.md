# Force Vector Directed Along a Line

## Force Along a Line

A force directed along a line can be expressed in **Cartesian vector form** using a unit vector along the line of action.

$$
\mathbf{\vec F}=F\mathbf{\vec u}
$$

where $F$ is the force magnitude and $\mathbf{\vec u}$ is the unit vector defining its direction.

## Unit Vector Along a Line

For a force directed from point $A$ to point $B$:

$$
\mathbf{\vec u}_{AB}=\frac{\mathbf{\vec r}_{AB}}{r_{AB}}
$$

where $\mathbf{r}_{AB}$ is the position vector from $A$ to $B$ and ${r}_{AB}$ is the magnitude.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\mathbf{\vec F}=F\frac{\mathbf{\vec r}_{AB}}{r_{AB}}}
$$

## Goofy Form

The following is the "long" form (not offical) name of the force vector $\mathbf{\vec{F}}$

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf{\vec F}
=
F\left(
\frac{x}{r_{AB}}\mathbf{i}
+

+

\right)
}
$$
